I'm **Walid Mawass**, an evolutionary geneticist and postdoctoral scholar with Jeremy Berg at the University of Chicago. I study the evolutionary processes that generate and maintain genetic variation in complex traits and fitness &mdash; mutation, selection, drift, and population history &mdash; and what that variation determines about the evolutionary response of populations and the interpretation of genetic association data.

Much of evolutionary and human genetics rests on quantities that are never observed directly: heritability, additive genetic variance, effective population size, and the ancestry components corrected for in genome-wide association studies. I use theory to derive the values these quantities should take under explicit models of mutation, selection, drift, and demography, and test whether empirical estimates are consistent with them. My work combines analytical quantitative and population genetics, random matrix theory, and forward-time simulation with multigenerational pedigrees of wild vertebrates, three centuries of French-Canadian genealogies, and biobank-scale genomic data.

## Research

- **Adaptation and persistence.** Why standing additive genetic variance often fails to predict the response to selection. In declining populations, the supply of beneficial mutations shrinks faster than deleterious load accumulates (mutational drought). Traits that are heritable and under consistent directional selection can still show evolutionary stasis. And evolutionary rescue after a population decline can leave the remaining genetic covariances misaligned with the direction of future selection.
- **The context-dependence of genetic effects.** An estimated allele effect is not a fixed, context-independent parameter; it is jointly shaped by ancestral background, assortative mating, parental genetic effects, environment, and survivorship. I develop theory to separate direct causal effects from this confounding: why statistically significant principal components can still leave population structure uncorrected in polygenic scores and tests for selection, how to distinguish gene-by-environment interaction from environmentally responsive genetic nurture, and whether the decline of genetic effect sizes with age reflects biology or survivorship bias.
- **The origins and maintenance of genetic variation.** What maintains additive genetic variance in fitness in natural populations, and how much of the observed deficit in neutral diversity it explains. How selection on disease liability shapes the genetic architecture of complex disease, and how polygenicity can arise without positive selection.
