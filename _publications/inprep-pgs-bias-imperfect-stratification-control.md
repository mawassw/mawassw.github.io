Principal component analysis is the workhorse correction for population stratification, but its behaviour in the regime that matters most — very large samples, many markers, diverse ancestry panels — is not what intuition suggests.

This project uses random matrix theory to characterize the limitations of PCA-based correction in large-scale genetic association studies, and quantifies the bias that residual ancestry stratification introduces into polygenic score associations and GWAS summary statistics.
